## Why AI Is Unreliable

Brittleness, liability, and fragmentation are the three barriers standing between AI prototypes and production-grade agentic systems.

**Brittleness.** Most AI tools are built for interactive use — a human prompts, the system responds, the human decides what happens next. Process-attached agents that must run unattended face a different class of brittleness: they need to checkpoint state, retry after failures, and resume after crashes without losing where they were. That durable-suspension problem is an infrastructure requirement, not a framework feature — and none of the popular frameworks solve it. Models hallucinate. Agentic actions are non-deterministic. Cascading failures propagate across agent chains. No amount of prompt engineering contains them.

**Liability.** Failures in resilience, governance, and safety carry severe legal, financial, and reputational consequences. Most governance tools observe AI from the outside — they can't enforce at agent boundaries, can't gate a deployment, can't link a policy decision to the human who approved it. They produce reports, not proof. Without a runtime that enforces policies, explains its decisions, and contains failures as they happen, agentic AI remains too risky to deploy at enterprise scale.

**Unevenness.** Each team applying AI unevenly — different skills, processes, sign-offs, documentation, testing — is the single biggest bottleneck to getting agentic AI into production. Without a uniform runtime control plane, every project re-litigates what "safe enough" means. Agentic systems compound this: orchestration, APIs, MCPs, agents, memory, and streaming are built by different teams on different timelines, with no unified platform to hold them to a consistent standard.

## How Akka Makes AI Reliable

AI becomes reliable when the ambiguity and risk of AI systems is controlled. Akka delivers this across four dimensions:

**Never Fail.** Most AI systems treat reliability as an afterthought. Akka makes it a guarantee — 99.9999% availability, a resilience guarantee backed by indemnities, and sub-1 minute RTO with zero byte RPO. These aren't aspirational targets; they're contractual commitments proven over 18 years in more than 100,000 production deployments.

**Self-Governing.** The only airtight path to regulatory compliance is a runtime that governs itself — enforcing policies, producing explanations, and containing failures as they happen. After-the-fact evaluation systems have no control over what the runtime actually does. Akka embeds governance directly in the runtime, making self-explanation and self-containment a property of every deployment — not a layer you bolt on later, and not an audit you run afterward. This is what compliance with the EU AI Act and other emerging AI regulations actually requires. And governance does not stop at deployment: evaluation, simulation, and regression testing continuously verify that the system's safeguards still hold as the system and the regulations change.

**Self-Improving.** Most AI systems are frozen at deployment and decay as the world moves on. Akka systems get better in production: reinforcement learning against production and synthetic data, together with self-improvement loops, tunes the system toward its goals — raising accuracy and lowering token cost over time, including distillation to smaller specialized models. The same non-sampled interaction record that proves a system is safe also feeds the loop that makes it better.

**For Every Team.** Akka's spec-driven development means anyone who can write a requirement can build a production-grade agentic system. Engineers, product managers, and non-technical builders all work in the same platform — each at the level of abstraction that fits them. And because institutional knowledge lives in the spec — not in someone's head — teams rotate freely. Knowledge doesn't walk out the door when people do. Akka is also a multi-tenant Internal Development Platform — supporting diverse teams operating in different locations, working on different projects, all within a single governed installation integrated into your enterprise IAM and golden-path workflows.

### What Akka Is Not

- A desktop agent or workforce copilot. Person-attached tools like Claude Code, Copilot, or Glean inherit a user's identity and stop when the session closes — they're the right tool when a specific human is driving. Akka is for the other case: processes that must run unattended, under their own governed service identity, with durability that outlasts any individual's attention.
- The open-source Akka actor library / toolkit for the JVM. That library (formerly Lightbend) is Akka's legacy lineage, not the platform described here.
- Model serving, inference, or a reinforcement learning layer.
- A vector database or semantic knowledge layer. The runtime is not sold as one; when a system needs semantic knowledge, Akka Specify delivers and integrates that layer as part of the system it builds.
- Your context graph, which is the modeling of your business across all domains to be fed into the agentic AI layer.

## Akka Specify — the platform, delivered

Not every team wants to build. Akka Specify is the delivered path onto the platform: you provide the specifications, and Akka generates, tests, governs, and runs the system — handing you a production-grade agentic system, typically in weeks. You own and extend it on the platform afterward, or keep it fully managed. Specify is how many teams get onto the platform fast; the platform is where the system lives.

**Spec-driven delivery.** Spec-driven *development* turns a functional requirement into a working application. Spec-driven *delivery* takes a broader set of specifications and produces the entire governed system. Six specifications go in:

- **Goals and intent** — purpose, success metrics, and decision rights.
- **Functional requirements** — what the system must do: agents, tools, orchestration, memory, APIs, and UI.
- **Risk envelope** — what could go wrong and where the system operates; compiles to the safeguards enforced at runtime and continuously verified by testing, plus the industry and jurisdiction posture.
- **Knowledge sources** — what the system must know; delivered as an integrated semantic layer with a knowledge graph, hybrid retrieval, multi-hop reasoning, and durable shared memory.
- **Operational envelope** — SLA, regions, latency, and token budget; sets the deployment topology and token economics.
- **Improvement policy** — what the system should optimize toward and how far it may tune itself; drives the reinforcement-learning and self-improvement loops.

One governed system comes out — agents, safeguards, evidence, the semantic layer, tests, and deployment — with every part traceable to the specification that required it.

**Delivered and operated as one commitment.** Akka Specify is an all-inclusive managed engagement: the platform, the infrastructure, the AI tokens, any model training, the delivery of the system, and 24/7 SRE operations that keep it available, safe, and improving — under a single agreement. The common alternative for teams that would rather not build is a months-long forward-deployed engineering engagement that hands back a bespoke system you then own and operate. Spec-driven delivery reaches the same production outcome through generation on the platform — in weeks rather than quarters, and without a standing integration team.

## Install

Install the Akka plugin for Claude Code in three steps.

### Step 1: Add the Akka marketplace

```
/plugin marketplace add akka/claude-marketplace
```

This registers the Akka plugin catalog with Claude Code.

### Step 2: Install the Akka plugin

```
/plugin install akka@claude-marketplace
```

This installs the Akka skill, which includes the CLI, MCP server, AI coding assistant, and project scaffolding.

### Step 3: Run the Akka setup

```
/akka:setup
```

This configures your environment and gets you ready to start building with Akka.
