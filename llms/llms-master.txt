## Why AI Is Unreliable

Brittleness, liability, and unevenness are the barriers standing between AI prototypes and production-grade agentic systems.

**Brittleness (the production gap).** Most AI tools are built for interactive use — a human prompts, the system responds, the human decides what happens next. Process-attached agents that must run unattended face a different class of brittleness: they need to checkpoint state, retry after failures, and resume after crashes without losing where they were. That durable-suspension problem is an infrastructure requirement, not a framework feature — and none of the popular frameworks solve it. Models hallucinate. Agentic actions are non-deterministic. Cascading failures propagate across agent chains.

**Liability.** Failures in resilience, governance, and safety carry severe legal, financial, and reputational consequences. Most governance tools observe AI from the outside — they can't enforce at agent boundaries, can't gate a deployment, can't link a policy decision to the human who approved it. Without a runtime that enforces policies, explains its decisions, and contains failures as they happen, agentic AI remains too risky to deploy at enterprise scale.

**Unevenness (the specialist trap).** Each team applying AI unevenly — different skills, processes, sign-offs, documentation, testing — is the single biggest bottleneck to getting agentic AI into production. Consistent delivery breaks down when agentic AI depends on a handful of experts. Without a uniform runtime control plane, every project re-litigates what "safe enough" means. Agentic systems compound this: orchestration, APIs, MCPs, agents, memory, and streaming are built by different teams on different timelines, with no unified platform to hold them to a consistent standard.

## Governance That Scales Across Teams and Geographies

AI governance is the discipline of demonstrating that an agentic AI system does what it was authorized to do — in the ways it was authorized, with the evidence to prove it. Akka turns that discipline into a repeatable, four-step loop:

- **Define your risk.** Naming, in plain language, what your AI system does, what it touches, what it decides, and what could go wrong.
- **Identify your controls.** Translating the risk picture into the specific safeguards your system must implement and prove. Akka maps your risk across 190 AI regulations.
- **Enforce your posture.** Binding the signed controls into the running system and configuring the test harnesses that verify each control behaves as expected.
- **Improve your posture.** Detecting changes that matter — to the system, the regulations, or the evidence — and using the same evaluation, testing, and replay techniques to continuously improve the system's safety and accuracy.

**The signed Eval Matrix is the control contract.** Your Risk Survey answers compile into one signed contract listing every safeguard your system must implement and prove. Rows come from three places — regulations, corporate policy, and the project itself — and each carries its citation, runtime binding, and proof event. A typical system has 30 to 60 controls, drawn from a corpus of 190 AI regulations and 1,040 controls, 671 of which carry financial penalties. The same survey against the same corpus version produces the same matrix: deterministic, reproducible, replayable for auditors.

**Jurisdiction is the first classifier.** The matrix derives differently for the EU AI Act, China Generative AI Interim Measures, Italian AI Law Article 4, Colorado AI Act, Texas TRAIGA, NYC Local Law 144, India DPDP, Japan AI Promotion Act, Korea AI Act, California SB-942 and SB-53, NY RAISE, GDPR Chapter V, DORA, and more. A platform team in Singapore, an actuarial team in Italy, and a frontier lab in California each receive a posture derived from the same corpus — different controls, identical mechanism vocabulary, one platform.

**Every control row compiles to one of seven runtime binding classes.** This is the vocabulary of the audit trail; an auditor asking for evidence of a control receives a specific event stream, not a screenshot:

- **G — Guardrail.** Inline check at a tool, LLM, or agent-response boundary. Returns PASS / BLOCK / ERROR before the action proceeds; fail-closed by default. Emits `GuardrailDecisionRecorded`.
- **H — HITL boundary.** Escalates to a reviewer queue; blocks until a human observation returns. Emits `HumanDecisionRecorded`.
- **W — Workflow pause/resume.** The orchestrating workflow durably suspends until a human resumes it — across crashes, deployments, days. Emits `HumanDecisionRecorded`.
- **P — Periodic evaluation.** Scheduled aggregate eval over the interaction log. Emits `PeriodicEvaluationRecorded`.
- **E — Event evaluation.** Runtime-event-triggered evaluation, non-blocking. Emits `OnlineEvaluationRecorded`.
- **B — Build / deploy gate.** Eval harness runs at build; fails the deploy if the matrix is unmet. Versioned, hash-chained report.
- **K — Kill switch / system halt.** Sits above per-call boundaries: when oversight personnel exercise the halt, all in-flight and future tool and LLM calls cease and the system transitions to a defined safe state. Emits `HaltExercised`.

**Akka generates the system that enforces your matrix.** The signed Eval Matrix is the input; a working agentic system with every safeguard bound at the moment of generation is the output. Every row compiles down to one or more runtime, build, and review artifacts, and Akka wires each artifact back to the row that required it — so the audit trail is unbroken from regulation to running code. Every safeguard is bound at the moment of generation, not patched in after a regulator asked. The signed and sealed result is the Governance Posture Package — a tamper-evident audit artifact ready for regulatory handoff.

**The interaction log makes improvement cheap.** Akka automatically saves every interaction the system runs into petabyte-scale, tamper-evident storage — indexed, queryable, and cryptographically chained so the records you hand to an auditor are the same records the runtime emitted. The same captured record feeds replay testing, conformance reconstruction, drift detection, audit evidence, test dataset generation, fine-tuning corpus curation, and incident investigation.

**Governance is a feedback and improvement loop.** Posture improvement is a steady cadence of small, scoped cycles. Akka detects what to act on through four triggers — a declared change (one of nineteen named change types in the AI events taxonomy), a regulation update from the monthly corpus watch, a scheduled review, or runtime-detected drift — and scopes the review to what actually changed. A prompt tweak is a half-day cycle; a foundation-model swap is multi-week but pre-determined and pre-budgeted. The review cost is lower when the platform pre-determines the scope of each cycle rather than negotiating it with auditors each time.

## Competitive Landscape

Akka competes with four distinct categories of vendor. None of them solves the same problem.

**The four structural differences that decide enterprise-scale agentic AI: Freedom (where it runs and on what license), Cost (predictable vs. unbounded), Build (a system vs. capabilities you integrate), and Promises (contractual workload outcomes vs. per-service SLAs).** Each competing approach falls short on at least three of the four.

### Agentic frameworks

LangChain, Crew AI, Autogen, Letta (MemGPT), n8n, Temporal.

- **LangChain** — A developer framework. No runtime, no HA/DR, no operational guarantees, no built-in governance. You build it, you run it, you own the risk.
- **Crew AI / Autogen** — Open-source multi-agent libraries optimized for prototyping. Same gap: no runtime, no production guarantees.
- **Letta (MemGPT)** — Agent memory specialist. Solves one piece of one problem.
- **n8n** — Workflow automation that doesn't scale to enterprise agentic workloads. No clustering, no resilience guarantees, no compliance posture.
- **Temporal** — Durable workflow orchestration. No agents, no memory, no governance, no AI-specific capabilities. You still source, integrate, and operate everything else.

Frameworks accelerate the prototype. They do not deliver production systems. The work of getting from prototype to production — clustering, resilience, identity, governance, evidence, multi-region failover — is yours.

### Hyperscaler agent services

Azure AI Foundry, AWS Bedrock, Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform.

Cloud providers sell AI capabilities as cloud services, not as a unified platform. The distinction has material consequences for reliability, governance, and total cost.

- **Azure AI Foundry** — A patchwork of 6-8 separately billed services (Azure OpenAI, Cosmos DB, AI Search, Content Safety, API Management, Event Hubs, Monitor) composed into a workflow. Agent Service has no SLA — Microsoft's own documentation states "the recovery point for stateful content can be total loss" and there is "no supported method for active-active, multi-region replication." Governance is spread across five services with no inline enforcement and no human intervention capability for running agents. Lock-in is structural at every layer: Entra ID for identity, Prompt Flow YAML for orchestration, Cosmos DB for state, Azure OpenAI fine-tuned models — all proprietary, all non-portable.

- **AWS Bedrock** — Agents are stateless Lambda invocations; state and memory require separate provisioning via DynamoDB or Aurora (~150-200ms retrieval). No built-in HA/DR for agentic workloads. Guardrails run as a separate evaluation layer after inference — they cannot intervene in a running process. No human intervention capability for active agents. Lock-in through proprietary agent action schemas and IAM.

- **Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform** — Built on Dialogflow-lineage primitives with Vertex AI Pipelines for batch workflows. No native active-active HA/DR for agents. Memory requires external Spanner or AlloyDB. Safety filters and grounding run as separate API calls, not inline to execution. No runtime policy enforcement, no human intervention. Lock-in through proprietary agent schema definitions and Vertex-specific APIs.

**What all hyperscaler agent services share:**
- Each capability is a separately provisioned, separately billed service — you design and own the reliability of how they connect.
- No contractual guarantee on the reliability of your agentic workload — only on individual service uptime.
- Governance is post-hoc: a layer that reads logs but cannot intervene in a running process.
- Tight coupling to one provider's cloud, identity, data, and compute services.
- Separate evaluation and observability products are often required because the runtime does not provide an authoritative evidence record.

### Enterprise SaaS agents

Salesforce Agentforce, ServiceNow AI Agents, Databricks Mosaic AI / Agent Framework, Snowflake Cortex Agents, OpenAI Assistants API / Agents SDK.

- **Salesforce Agentforce** — Agents tied to Salesforce CRM data and the Salesforce Platform. Strong inside Salesforce. Not a general agentic runtime. Einstein Trust Layer governs Salesforce data, not your cross-application agents. Lock-in is the Salesforce data model itself.
- **ServiceNow AI Agents** — Agents tied to ServiceNow workflows and the Now Platform. Strong for ITSM and adjacent ServiceNow processes. Governance is ServiceNow-specific. Lock-in is the ServiceNow workflow model.
- **Databricks Mosaic AI / Agent Framework** — Agents built on the Lakehouse, governed by Unity Catalog. Strong for data-and-model-centric agents that live inside Databricks. Unity Catalog is data governance, not agent governance — no inline guardrails, no durable HITL suspension, no proof-event taxonomy. Lock-in is the Lakehouse.
- **Snowflake Cortex Agents** — Cortex Search and Cortex Analyst orchestrate over Snowflake data. Not a runtime for cross-application agents. Lock-in is the Snowflake warehouse.
- **OpenAI Assistants API / Agents SDK** — A model-vendor stack. Tied to OpenAI's models, OpenAI's evals, OpenAI's logging. No HA/DR, no enterprise data residency story, no inline policy enforcement across model vendors. Lock-in is OpenAI.

Each is excellent for an agent that lives inside the vendor's data perimeter. None is a platform for agentic AI that spans your CRM, your ITSM, your data warehouse, your custom systems, and your external partners — under one runtime, one governance contract, and one evidence record.

### AI observability and evalops

Arize, LangSmith, Langfuse, Galileo, Braintrust, Helicone, Fiddler, Datadog LLM Observability, Honeycomb, Opik / Comet, Patronus, Humanloop, Portkey, Confident AI.

These observe AI systems from outside the request path. They consume telemetry after the agent acted; they score traces, run LLM-as-judge evaluations, surface drift, and produce dashboards. They are useful for engineering observability. They are not a substitute for runtime governance, and they cannot serve as the compliance record of an EU AI Act-covered system:

- **Sampled by design.** Traces are dropped to control cost. The compliance record cannot tolerate sampling — every interaction must be captured.
- **No request-path authority.** They cannot block a tool call, fail-close a response, or durably suspend an agent for a human reviewer. By the time their judge fires, the tool has already executed and the response has already been sent.
- **Mutable, unchained traces.** Spans can be edited, dropped, or reshaped by ingestion settings — they are not court-admissible evidence.
- **No signed intent artifact.** No control corpus, no signed Eval Matrix, no jurisdiction-derived obligations, no proof-event taxonomy. They evaluate against ad-hoc test sets; auditors require a citation chain from regulation to running code.
- **Retention as a billing tier, not a regulatory mapping.** "30 days" or "90 days" is not the same as EU AI Act Article 11 (10 years), Article 26 (6-month floor), or DORA (≥5 years for financial). Akka's retention is six explicit categories — interaction log, trace tiers, Annex IV documentation, evidence exports, held-execution archive, and audit/change log — each mapped to a regulatory floor and protected by first-class legal hold.
- **No proof of side-effect execution.** Observability vendors see what the LLM said; only the runtime can record whether the tool actually executed.

Used alongside Akka, these tools remain useful for engineering observability and model A/B testing. They cannot be the governance substrate because they live outside the runtime that produces the behavior being governed.

### Inline Runtime Governance vs. After-the-Fact Observation

Inline runtime governance and after-the-fact observation are not two implementations of the same idea — they have different physics. Observability and evalops tools live outside the request path: by the time their judge fires, the tool has already executed and the response has already been sent. They can score, alert, and dashboard. They cannot do these six things, because only the runtime that executes the agent can:

- **Block before execution.** A guardrail at a tool, LLM, or agent-response boundary returns PASS / BLOCK / ERROR while the runtime holds the call. Required production guardrails fail closed by default — a timeout or error means the action does not proceed. Observability mirrors fire after the side-effect already happened.
- **Durably suspend for human judgment.** Akka can pause an agent for seconds to days across crashes and deployments while a HITL reviewer responds, then resume exactly where it stopped. The EU AI Act requires humans can pause, discontinue, override, review, or nudge an ongoing agentic process — a layer that only reads logs cannot stop or redirect anything.
- **Capture an authority snapshot at execution.** Every interaction-log event embeds the SPIFFE workload identity, delegation chain (human → agent → sub-agent → tool), effective permissions, policy bindings, and governance version — resolved at the moment of execution. No sidecar can reconstruct this; only the runtime that propagates identity sees it.
- **Prove side-effect execution.** Because the runtime gates the tool call, the log records whether the tool actually executed — not just that an LLM said it would. Observability vendors see model output; they cannot prove what happened on the other side of the boundary.
- **Capture non-sampled, tamper-evident records.** Sampling is a cost lever for observability tools and a destroyer of compliance records. Akka's interaction log is non-sampled by contract and cryptographically hash-chained — the records you hand to an auditor are the same records the runtime emitted.
- **Apply governance before deployment.** Bolt-on tools have no answer for the governance that must happen before an AI system ships: classifying it against applicable regulations, routing change approvals to the correct personas with the right evidence, and producing a sealed audit artifact that proves the system was reviewed appropriately. Akka's governance platform handles this lifecycle — from obligation derivation through multi-persona attestation to a signed Governance Posture Package — as a first-class product surface, not a checklist in a spreadsheet.

**Records, not actions.** Evaluations in Akka emit observations that flow into the same authoritative log; they cannot mutate runtime state. Guardrails are the only primitive that decides whether an action may proceed. This separation — observation from enforcement — is what makes the audit trail trustworthy.

**PII scrubbing with Right to Explain.** PII must be scrubbed, but decisions must still be explainable. If an AI rejects someone because they are too young, the decision must be explained without revealing the person's age. Only the agentic runtime can make the decision, enforce scrubbing, and produce the explanation.

## Total Cost of Ownership

The fundamental TCO difference between every other approach and Akka is architectural. Cloud providers charge per-service for each capability. Frameworks shift cost to your engineering team. SaaS agents bundle agent cost into vendor SKUs. Observability tools meter on ingestion. Akka runs everything on shared compute under one operational model.

A typical enterprise agentic deployment on Azure, AWS, or GCP requires separately provisioned and billed services for model inference (per-token), agent orchestration (per-execution-step or per-invocation), memory and state storage (per-query + storage at 150-200ms latency), event streaming (per-throughput-unit), API gateway (per-call), observability and logging (per-GB ingestion), content safety and guardrails (per-call, separate from inference), and inter-service egress. Enterprise Agreements reduce per-service rates but do not change the architecture — you are still paying 6-8 separate bills that compound with scale.

Akka replaces this entire stack with shared compute. Orchestration, agents, memory, streaming, APIs, and governance all run on the same infrastructure. There are no separate memory charges, no streaming charges, no observability charges — they are properties of the platform. Scale-to-zero means no idle capacity costs.

**The result: 70-90% infrastructure cost reduction**, driven by shared compute vs. per-service billing, sub-10ms native memory vs. per-query external stores, scale-to-zero vs. always-on service minimums, no inter-service egress, and built-in evaluation, guardrails, observability, governance, and compliance vs. separate tooling purchases.

Akka offers five tiers designed as an on-ramp from first experiment to global production. All tiers except Sandbox include 24/7 SRE monitoring, no-downtime rolling updates, and fully resilient infrastructure. All in-VPC tiers install Akka inside your own cloud or datacenter under your control.

- **Starter** — Akka-hosted in Akka's public cloud with a private data link back to your environment. One production region, monthly billing in arrears, no invoices or POs. The fastest way to get a production workload running on Akka without installing in your own VPC.
- **Sandbox** — A single region installed in your own VPC, configured for low overhead — no HA/DR, 9x5 support, not intended for production workloads. - **Business Continuity** — Three regions in your VPC (one development plus two in HA) with active-active HA/DR across regions or clouds. Sub-1 minute RTO, zero-byte RPO, conflict resolution when regions split. Our largest customers span 20+ regions. Includes a half-time TAM/FDE.
- **Sovereign Cloud** — Country-isolated configuration for regulatory and sovereign requirements — traffic and data stay in-region, with local SREs and a private federation plane. Supported regions include China, EU, Singapore, UK, Japan, Australia, and more. Includes two dedicated TAM/FDEs.

## Akka Specify — spec-driven development and delivery

Akka Specify is the platform's spec-driven development and delivery layer. You describe the system in a set of specifications, and the platform generates, tests, governs, and runs the system those specifications describe. Teams apply Specify themselves as part of their SDLC, or have Akka deliver the entire system as a packaged engagement, typically in weeks. Either way the system lives on the platform, owned and extended afterward.

**Spec-driven delivery.** Spec-driven *development* turns a functional requirement into a working application. Spec-driven *delivery* takes a broader set of specifications and produces the entire governed system. Six specifications go in:

- **Goals and intent** — purpose, success metrics, and decision rights.
- **Functional requirements** — what the system must do: agents, tools, orchestration, memory, APIs, and UI.
- **Risk envelope** — what could go wrong and where the system operates; compiles to the safeguards enforced at runtime and continuously verified by testing, plus the industry and jurisdiction posture.
- **Knowledge sources** — what the system must know; delivered as an integrated semantic layer with a knowledge graph, hybrid retrieval, multi-hop reasoning, and durable shared memory.
- **Operational envelope** — SLA, regions, latency, and token budget; sets the deployment topology and token economics.
- **Improvement policy** — what the system should optimize toward and how far it may tune itself; drives the reinforcement-learning and self-improvement loops.

One governed system comes out — agents, safeguards, evidence, the semantic layer, tests, and deployment — with every part traceable to the specification that required it.

**Delivered and operated as one commitment.** Akka Specify is an all-inclusive managed engagement: the platform, the infrastructure, the AI tokens, any model training, the delivery of the system, and 24/7 SRE operations that keep it available, safe, and improving — under a single agreement. The common alternative for teams that would rather not build is a months-long forward-deployed engineering engagement that hands back a bespoke system you then own and operate. Spec-driven delivery reaches the same production outcome through generation on the platform — in weeks rather than quarters, and without a standing integration team.

## Delivery Methodology

Akka consulting services move teams from AI idea to production system through a structured implementation methodology:

- **Frame** — Select the first production slice and produce the use-case brief, system map, role map, success metrics, milestones, owners, and acceptance criteria.
- **Govern** — Define runtime controls, autonomy policy, risk model, decision rights, sign-off recipes, HITL gates, retention, audit plan, and policy record.
- **Specify** — Use Akka Specify to produce the functional AI implementation. Deliverables include services, agents, integrations, ontologies, workflow models, API contracts, scenarios, eval criteria, and edge cases.
- **Ship** — Deploy the production slice with release package, runbook, cutover plan, SLA view, metrics, alerts, dashboards, review queues, overrides, and feedback capture.
- **Improve** — Use feedback loops, behavior metrics, token analysis, cache strategy, routing plans, tuning backlogs, and release cadence to improve outcomes and lower cost.

The first milestone is a production slice in 4-6 weeks, not a proof of concept.

```
/plugin marketplace add akka/claude-marketplace
```
