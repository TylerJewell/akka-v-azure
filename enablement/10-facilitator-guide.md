# Facilitator guide

How to run each session, what to prepare, and the failure modes to watch for.

---

## Before any session

Read the Akka positioning reference end to end. Every number a learner will be assessed on
comes from it, and a facilitator who cannot cite the source cannot mark a fact check.

Resolve the four figure conflicts in `README.md` §Facts to reconcile before running the fact
check. Marking a learner wrong for quoting a figure the source material carries loses a room quicker than anything else.

## Session plans

### Foundation, 90 minutes live

| Minutes | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0–15 | The four-cost table on a whiteboard, no slides. Each participant writes it from memory at minute 12. |
| 15–35 | The efficiency thesis and the mechanism. Ask why cloud providers cannot match the single runtime, and do not answer it for them. |
| 35–55 | What Akka is not, and the disqualifiers. Run a round of "would you take this meeting" with five inbound descriptions, two of which are disqualifiers. |
| 55–80 | The 90-second statement. Every person delivers it. Time it out loud. |
| 80–90 | Assign the fact check as self-paced work. |

Most participants run 2 to 3 minutes on their first attempt. The cut is always the mechanism
sentences, and keeping the four proofs is what matters.

### Solution modules, 2 hours live each

| Minutes | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0–20 | The cost, the owner, and the customer challenge. Participants name a real account that matches. |
| 20–50 | Capabilities and mechanism. Field CTOs go deeper here; AEs move on. |
| 50–75 | Differentiators. Run the concession first, then the contrast, out loud, in pairs. |
| 75–100 | The quick wins. Each participant picks one and states how they would propose it. |
| 100–120 | The discovery call assessment, run in threes. |

### Routing, 2 hours live

| Minutes | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0–25 | The two inputs and the resolution rule. |
| 25–45 | The second-cost rule, and the eight opening sentences practised aloud. |
| 45–60 | The five routing failures, with participants naming which one they are most likely to commit. |
| 60–100 | The drill. Read each cold open aloud, allow 30 seconds, take the answer, move on. Do not debrief items individually. |
| 100–120 | Debrief the whole drill at once, item by item, with the signal column. |

Reading the cold opens aloud matters. Handing out the table lets learners pattern-match on its structure instead of on what a person said.

### Mandate owners, 2 hours live

| Minutes | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0–20 | Why cost routing fails, and the two failure shapes. |
| 20–45 | The four archetypes. Participants classify three real people they have met this year. |
| 45–60 | The four classification questions, practised until they sound conversational rather than interrogative. |
| 60–75 | The heat question, and why annoyance locates budget. |
| 75–120 | The certification meeting, run in threes. |

Participants resist the workforce-transformer disqualifier. Hold the line. Claiming Akka does
copilots costs more deals than it wins.

### Competitive, 2 hours live

Three role plays, 15 minutes each, plus debrief. Facilitators must prepare the matching
battlecard and comparison page and must be able to source any claim a learner uses. A learner
who cites something unsourced should be asked where it comes from, in the role play.

### Scenarios, 2 hours each

Groups of three: learner, facilitator, observer. Rotate.

## Playing cast members

**Concede nothing the learner did not ask for.** Every scenario lists hidden facts and the question that unlocks each. Volunteering them removes the exercise.

**Play the resentment honestly.** Tomas defending LangGraph, Amara expecting an ambush, Halima
protecting her budget, and Priyanka defending her mandate are all correct behaviour by people
doing their jobs. Learners who never meet resistance in training meet it first in front of a
customer.

**Let bad branches run.** A learner on a losing branch should feel the meeting stall. Ten
minutes of a polite, useless conversation teaches more than an interruption.

**Deliver the recovery prompt once, and only where the scenario specifies it.**

**Never break character to teach.** Everything goes into the debrief.

## The failure modes to watch for

| Failure | Where it shows | The correction |
|---|---|---|
| The platform pitch | Any first meeting | Ask which sentence the customer said that the learner was answering |
| Quoting Akka's percentages instead of the customer's number | Business-case discussions | Ask what the delay costs per month, and note that the customer had the figure |
| Attacking the incumbent | Competitive role plays | Replay the exchange with the concession first |
| Naming a second cost owned by someone present | End of every meeting | Ask who the new meeting is with |
| Forecasting on a champion with no budget | Scenario 7 | Ask what that person can approve without asking anyone |
| Overclaiming an absolute | Everywhere | Ask for the source sentence |
| Selling the mechanism before the cost | Solution modules | Ask whether the customer has agreed they have the problem |

Selling the mechanism before the cost is the most common failure and the least noticed. Shared
compute, the Eval Matrix, and the six specifications are all mechanisms, and a mechanism
described before an agreed problem is a product tour.

## Materials

The program renders as two documents. Participants get the first and never the second.

- **The participant edition** carries the modules and, for each scenario, the account brief
  and the cast. Branch outcomes, hidden cast facts, scoring sheets, debriefs, the
  routing-drill answers, and this guide are all absent from it.
- **The facilitator edition** carries everything, including every answer key. Distribute it
  to nobody who will sit in a session as a learner.

Per session: whiteboard, the participant edition, and for competitive and scenarios the
matching battlecards, comparison pages, and case studies.

## Running this for partners

Partners take foundation, every solution module, routing, mandate owners, and scenarios 8 and 9, then the partner track. Two changes:

- Partner staff turn over faster than the annual recertification cadence assumes. Track each
  individual by name alongside the firm.
- Scenario 5 is required, and facilitating it is the assessment. A partner who has facilitated
  the partner-conflict failure does not commit it.

Send partners the participant edition. The facilitator edition carries the answer keys that make the scenarios worth running.

Both editions are published on the public site, so the split between them controls what a partner is handed. The split does not control what a partner could find. Write accordingly: anything that
would damage a deal if a customer or a competitor read it does not belong in either file.
