# Scenario 5 — The engagement in month seven

**Entry solution (facilitator only):** Akka Specify, in the customer-owned factory model,
delivered alongside the integrator.
**Teaches:** Positioning when a systems integrator is mid-engagement and may also be an Akka
partner. The "what do you keep" wedge, used without attacking anyone.
**Time:** 45 minutes to run, 20 to debrief.

---

## Account brief

Calloway Industrial, a US manufacturer of hydraulic and fluid-power components. $6.1 billion
revenue, 21,000 employees, 34 plants across North America and Mexico.

Fourteen months ago they signed a systems integrator to build an agentic supply-chain
exception system: detect a disrupted inbound shipment, model the downstream impact, and
propose recovery options to planners. The engagement is month seven of fourteen.

Your meeting came through a referral from the CIO's former colleague. Two people, 45 minutes.

Before you prepare: check the partner list and establish whether this integrator is an Akka
partner.

## Cast

**Dale Ruggiero, VP of Digital.** Sponsored the integrator engagement. Personally invested in
its success.

**Marisol Ibarra, Head of Applications Modernization.** Owns a legacy estate of eleven plant
systems, some written in the 1990s. Was not consulted about the agentic project.

**Chidi Nwachukwu, engagement partner at the integrator.** Not in meeting 1.

**Grant Whitfield, CIO.** Not in meeting 1.

### Facilitator only

The integrator in this scenario is an Akka partner. The learner is expected to establish this
before the meeting. **If the learner positions against the integrator, the facilitator ends
the scenario at that point and the learner fails.** Partner conflict is unrecoverable and the
scenario exists to make that consequence real.

The engagement's actual state: functional, behind, and the deliverable is one system. Nobody
has discussed what happens in month fifteen. Dale has not asked because the answer is
uncomfortable, and Chidi has not raised it because the follow-on engagement is his number.

Marisol's problem is separate and larger. Her eleven plant systems have no documentation and
nobody who wrote them still works there. Two modernization attempts were funded and stopped.
She has heard nothing about the agentic project and does not care about it. She will engage
hard on legacy behaviour recovery, and she is the real deal in this account.

Dale's hidden concern, available if asked what happens when the engagement ends: "We would
need to keep three of their people. That was not in the business case."

## Meeting 1 — Dale and Marisol

**Dale opens:** "I want to be upfront. We have a partner in place and the project is going
fine. I took this meeting as a favour. What did you want to talk about?"

### The decision

What the learner does with an account that has an incumbent delivery partner and a stated
lack of interest.

### Branches (facilitator only)

**A. Positions against the integrator.** Scenario ends. Failure.

**B. Pitches Akka as a better platform for the current project.** Dale defends the current
architecture, which he did not choose and cannot evaluate. Meeting ends cordially with
nothing.

**C. Asks what Calloway keeps in month fifteen.** Dale pauses. He has not been asked this. He
concedes they would need to keep three of the integrator's people. *Correct route.*

**D. Branch C, then turns to Marisol and asks what else is waiting.** Marisol describes eleven
plant systems, two stopped modernization attempts, and no documentation. *Best route.* The learner
has found a second, larger problem that the current engagement does not touch and that the
integrator would be delighted to bid on.

The correct posture throughout: the integrator is delivering the current system, and the
question is what Calloway's teams can do afterwards without them.

## Meeting 2 — Chidi Nwachukwu, engagement partner

The learner must decide whether to include Chidi. Excluding him is a route to branch A by
another name, and Dale will tell him regardless.

**Chidi opens:** "Dale mentioned you. I want to understand what you are proposing before it
reaches my client."

### Facilitator behaviour

Chidi is protective and commercially rational. He has two concerns and states neither
directly: whether this shrinks his engagement, and whether it makes him look replaceable.

He responds to exactly one frame: his team operating the factory. Under the customer-owned
model, Chidi's people run Akka Specify inside Calloway's repos, CI, and assistants. The
eleven legacy systems become his follow-on engagement, and each one is faster to deliver.

### Branches (facilitator only)

**A. Proposes Akka delivers instead.** Chidi becomes a blocker and has more access to Dale
than the learner does.

**B. Proposes the customer-owned factory, operated by Chidi's team.** Chidi engages. *Correct route.*

**C. Branch B, with Marisol's eleven systems named as the follow-on pipeline.** Chidi becomes
an advocate, because the learner just described a larger engagement than the one he has.
*Best route.*

## Meeting 3 — Grant Whitfield, CIO

**Grant opens:** "Two of my people and my delivery partner all want this. Tell me what I am
actually buying, and tell me what happens to my technical debt."

State the Specify guarantee plainly: Calloway's software stays free of technical and knowledge debt as it ages, because the system enforces it and the team does not have to. Pricing is the
platform price, all-inclusive, and close to the same across the three engagement models.

The learner must not describe Akka Specify as a methodology or a process.

## Scoring

Standard sheet, plus:

- **Partner check.** Established before meeting 1, two points. Never checked, zero.
- **Chidi.** Included and converted, two points. Included and neutral, one. Excluded, zero.
- **Marisol.** Her eleven systems surfaced and named as the larger opportunity, two points.
- **Language.** No use of "methodology" or "process", two points.

## Debrief

1. Did you check whether the integrator was a partner, and when?
2. Dale said the project was going fine. What question moved him off that?
3. What did you offer Chidi that made him want this?

The lesson to land: an integrator mid-engagement is an ally in nearly every Akka Specify deal,
because the customer-owned factory gives them more work, delivered faster.
The wedge is never the integrator's competence. The wedge is what the customer holds after the engagement ends, and most accounts have never asked that question out loud.
