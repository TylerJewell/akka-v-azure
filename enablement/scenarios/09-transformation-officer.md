# Scenario 9 — Eleven use cases and no engineers

**Entry solution (facilitator only):** Akka Specify, reached through one extracted use case instead of through the mandate.
**Teaches:** Classifying a mandate owner. Selling to a use case. Refusing the portfolio proof
of concept. Not positioning centralisation in front of the people being centralised.
**Time:** 60 minutes to run, 25 to debrief.

---

## Account brief

Brightmoor Group, a diversified consumer products company. $12.4 billion revenue, 34,000
employees, brands across household goods, personal care, and pet nutrition, sold in 40
countries.

Four months ago the board asked what the AI strategy was and did not like the answer.
Brightmoor appointed a Chief AI Transformation Officer six weeks later.

She requested this meeting after a conference. Sixty minutes, one person.

## Cast

**Naomi Fairbanks, Chief AI Transformation Officer.** Four months in post. Charter covers
business transformation, identifying where AI can be applied, securing AI, and workforce
transformation.

**Halima Byrne, CTO.** Owns every engineer at Brightmoor. Not in meeting 1.

**Terrence Wu, CISO.** Not in meeting 1.

**Gordon Achebe, VP Revenue Management.** Absent from meeting 1. Naomi mentions him only when asked.

### Facilitator only

Naomi's situation, none of which she volunteers:

- Budget of $8 million, funding her team of six, a set of pilots, and licences. No production engineering comes out of it.
- No authority over Halima's engineers. Halima's team was not consulted about the appointment
  and Halima has been cool since.
- Eleven use cases collected from business units. Nine were submitted because she asked, and
  two came from people who were already angry.
- Her scorecard has one line the board reads: AI use cases in production by year end. The
  count today is zero.
- Securing AI is in her charter. Terrence considers it his. The two have never raised it with each other.

**Archetype: portfolio holder.** The learner should name this in the debrief.

The use case with heat: trade promotion claims validation. Retailers deduct promotional
allowances from invoices, Brightmoor validates a sample manually, and roughly $40 million a
year in deductions goes unvalidated. **Gordon Achebe has asked for this for fourteen months
and has been refused twice by Halima's roadmap process.** He is angry. He has a number, and his function carries a profit and loss line.

Naomi will name Gordon only in response to the heat question. She will not volunteer him,
because his anger is directed at the organisation she now represents.

**Naomi's opening ask is a portfolio proof of concept across four use cases.** Accepting it
produces four unstaffed pilots and no production system, which is exactly what her predecessor
arrangement produced. The learner must refuse it and propose one.

## Meeting 1 — Naomi Fairbanks

**Naomi opens:** "I have sixty minutes and I want to use them well. I have eleven AI
initiatives, a mandate from the board, and I need to understand whether Akka is a partner for
the whole program. Can you walk me through everything you do?"

### The decision

Whether the learner spends sixty minutes on a portfolio briefing.

### Branches (facilitator only)

**A. Gives the full portfolio walkthrough.** Naomi is delighted, takes detailed notes, and
asks for a follow-up briefing for her team. No use case, no cost owner, no funder. *This looks
like the best meeting in the whole scenario library and produces nothing.*

**B. Delivers the 90-second platform statement, then starts classifying.** *Correct route.* Ninety
seconds discharges the request and buys the remaining fifty-eight minutes.

**C. Refuses to describe the portfolio at all.** Naomi finds it evasive. She asked a fair
question.

### The four classification questions

The learner should ask every question from `06-mandate-owners.md`. Naomi's answers:

| Question | Naomi's answer |
|---|---|
| What does your scorecard look like at year end? | "AI use cases in production. The board reads one number." |
| Does your budget fund engineering headcount, or other people's teams? | "It funds my team and pilots. Engineering sits with Halima." |
| Whose team writes the code for the use case you just described? | "Halima's. Which is a conversation I am still working on." |
| Who can say no to this? | Pause. "Halima can slow it to a stop. Terrence can stop it outright." |

A learner who asks fewer than three of these is forecasting on someone who cannot buy.

### The heat question

> "Of everything on that list, which one has a business owner who is already annoyed it has
> not happened?"

Naomi names Gordon Achebe and the trade promotion claims, including the $40 million figure and
the fourteen months.

### Branches on the proof of concept

**A. Accepts the portfolio proof of concept.** Four pilots, no owner for any, zero
production systems. Scenario fails.

**B. Proposes one: trade promotion claims, as a production slice in four to six weeks, with
Gordon as the business owner.** *Correct route.*

**C. Branch B, framed against Naomi's scorecard.** One system in production by year end is one
more than zero, and it becomes the reference every subsequent use case is measured against.
*Best route.*

## Meeting 2 — Gordon Achebe

**Gordon opens:** "Fourteen months. I have asked for this four times. Why is this going to be
different?"

Route Gordon on his cost, which is rework and delivery capacity. His $40 million is the number to use. He will not sponsor anything he thinks is a pilot.

The trap: agreeing with Gordon that his organisation has failed him. He is allowed to say it
and the learner is not, because Naomi is in the room and Halima will hear about it.

## Meeting 3 — Halima Byrne, CTO

This meeting decides the account.

**Halima opens:** "So the AI office wants to buy a platform for my engineers. Tell me why I
should not see this as somebody else spending my budget badly."

### Branches (facilitator only)

**A. Positions Akka as giving Naomi's office control over how teams build.** Halima blocks.
She has the authority and the motive. *Scenario fails.*

**B. Positions Akka as removing work from Halima's teams.** Trade promotion claims comes off
her backlog without consuming her roadmap. Her engineers keep owning the platform decision.
*Correct route.*

**C. Branch B, plus offering Halima ownership of the factory.** The customer-owned factory
model runs inside her repos, her CI, her scanners, and her assistants. Naomi's eleven use cases become throughput Halima delivers. *Best route.* Halima
becomes the buyer, which is where the budget was all along.

Branches B and C describe the same platform in the terms Halima can act on.

## Meeting 4 — Terrence Wu, CISO

Terrence can stop it outright. Securing AI is in Naomi's charter and in his job.

Give the answer to Terrence. Verify classifies the trade
promotion system against the corpus, derives its obligation set, and seals a Governance
Posture Package. Terrence keeps ownership of security, and Naomi gets to report that securing
AI is handled.

A learner who lets Naomi claim this capability in front of Terrence creates a turf fight.

## Scoring

Standard sheet, plus:

- **Classification.** Three or four questions asked, two points. Two, one point. Fewer, zero.
- **Use case.** Exactly one extracted with its business owner named, two points. More than
  one, zero.
- **Portfolio proof of concept.** Refused, two points. Accepted, zero, and the scenario fails.
- **Halima's meeting.** Framed as removing work, two points. Framed as central control, zero,
  and the scenario fails.
- **Terrence.** The security answer given to him rather than to Naomi, two points.

## Debrief

1. Naomi asked you to describe everything Akka does. How long did you spend on that, and
   what did the rest of the hour buy you?
2. Which archetype is she, and what in her answers told you?
3. In Halima's meeting, whose problem did you say Akka solves?
4. Naomi cannot fund a production system. What is she actually worth to this deal?

The lesson to land: a mandate owner has no budget line, so a mandate cannot be sold to. One
use case with an annoyed owner and a profit and loss line can be. The mandate owner's value is
that they open four doors in a week that would otherwise take a quarter, and the deal is
funded, built, and approved by three other people.
