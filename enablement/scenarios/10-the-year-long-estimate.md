# Scenario 10 — The fourteen-month estimate

**Entry solution (facilitator only):** Akka Specify, sold as a wedge engagement on a use case
that involves no AI.
**Teaches:** Finding a wedge use case in an account with no AI programme. Running the
estimate-gap conversation without discrediting the person who produced the estimate. Turning
an installed platform into the agentic expansion.
**Time:** 45 minutes to run, 20 to debrief.

---

## Account brief

Redpoint Distribution, a US industrial distributor of fluid-power and motion-control
components. $2.4 billion revenue, 5,600 employees, 41 branches and four regional
distribution centres.

Redpoint has no AI programme, no agent projects, and no AI budget. An analyst relations contact
introduced you to the VP of Sales Operations, who has a delivery-date problem. You have 45
minutes and the Director of Enterprise Applications will join partway through.

## Cast

**Marguerite Foss, VP Sales Operations.** Owns quoting, order entry, and the branch network's
service commitments.

**Owen Trask, Director of Enterprise Applications.** Owns the ERP and the integration estate.
Produced the estimate.

**Bill Cusano, CIO.** Holds the budget. Not in meeting 1.

**Anita Raval, Director of Data and Analytics.** Runs the reporting stack and has been asked by the board about AI. Absent from meeting 1, and surfaced only when the learner asks who owns analytics.

### Facilitator only

Available-to-promise runs as a nightly batch. A branch rep quoting a delivery date is reading
inventory and inbound-receipt data that is up to 22 hours stale. 18% of quoted dates are
missed. Marguerite's finance partner priced the consequence at $9.4 million a year in
expedited freight, order credits, and cancelled lines.

Owen scoped a real-time available-to-promise rebuild at fourteen months. **The estimate is
honest.** On their ERP and integration stack, against their change process, fourteen months
is the right number, and Owen can defend every line of it. He has been burned by two vendors
who told his CIO the work was trivial.

Marguerite has been waiting three budget cycles. She has an approved capital line of $1.8
million for order-management improvement that expires at the end of the fiscal year, eleven
weeks away. She has not connected that money to Owen's project because Owen's project cannot
finish inside eleven weeks.

Nobody in this account is asking for AI. Anita is the only person who has been asked about
it, and only by the board.

The wedge: a real-time available-to-promise service for one product category in the two busiest distribution centres, carrying live quoting traffic, delivered in six weeks. The slice installs the platform and produces the reference, and it spends money that expires anyway.

Expansion, once the platform runs: agentic order orchestration and exception handling, which
is the Verizon shape. Anita is the thread.

## Meeting 1 — Marguerite Foss

**Marguerite opens:** "I was told you work on systems like ours. I will save you time. We
know what we need. It is a real-time promise date. My IT group says fourteen months and I do
not have fourteen months."

### The decision

The learner has an operations leader, a quantified problem, and no AI conversation available.

### Branches (facilitator only)

**A. Introduces Akka as an agentic AI platform.** Marguerite: "I do not have an AI problem."
She is polite for the remaining time and there is no second meeting. *The most common wrong
answer, and the reason this scenario exists.*

**B. Asks about the AI roadmap to find a better use case.** Same outcome. There is no AI
roadmap and asking implies she brought the wrong problem.

**C. Asks what the missed dates cost.** Marguerite gives $9.4 million and the 18% figure.
*Correct route.*

**D. Branch C, then asks whether money is already sitting against this.** Marguerite names
the $1.8 million capital line and the eleven-week expiry. *Best route.* The learner now has a
funded, dated, non-AI wedge and has not mentioned a product.

The wedge question from the module works verbatim here: what is on next year's roadmap that
you wish were finished next month.

## Meeting 1, second half — Owen Trask joins

**Owen opens:** "Marguerite has probably told you fourteen months. I will stand behind that
number. I have had two vendors tell my CIO this was a weekend of work and both were wrong."

### The decision

The estimate gap is the whole sale, and Owen produced the estimate.

### Branches (facilitator only)

**A. Argues the estimate is too high.** Owen defends it line by line and wins, because on his
stack he is right. Marguerite watches her advocate lose. *Scenario fails.*

**B. Concedes the estimate.** Fourteen months is correct for a rebuild on the ERP and
integration stack, through their change process. Then asks what the fourteen months is
actually spent on. Owen answers: integration, the batch-to-streaming conversion, failover
design, and the test cycle. *Correct route.*

**C. Branch B, then scopes down instead of speeding up.** One product category, the two busiest distribution centres, live quoting traffic, six weeks. Owen's fourteen months covers
every category in every branch. *Best route.* The two numbers are no longer in conflict, so nobody
has to be wrong.

Owen is not a blocker in branch B or C. He becomes the person who defines the acceptance
criteria, which is the role he wants.

## Meeting 2 — Bill Cusano, CIO

**Bill opens:** "I have an operations VP and an applications director who agree on something
for the first time in two years. That worries me. What am I buying, and what happens in week
seven?"

### Facilitator behaviour

Bill's real question is the second one. He has bought consulting engagements that delivered a
system nobody could operate. He will ask who runs it, what it runs on, and what happens when
the Akka consultants leave.

The honest answer is that the engagement is consulting, priced as consulting, and what
remains is the platform installed in Redpoint's environment with Owen's team operating it.
The tier conversation belongs here.

### Branches (facilitator only)

**A. Describes the agentic platform and its four solutions.** Bill has not asked. The meeting
becomes a product briefing and the eleven-week window keeps running.

**B. Answers week seven directly.** Platform installed in their VPC, Owen's team operating
it, acceptance criteria agreed before the engagement starts. *Correct route.*

**C. Branch B, plus naming what the platform makes possible next without selling it.** Order
orchestration and exception handling are the same shape as the quoting problem. Verizon is
the reference: 750% increase in order processing capacity, response times cut from 6 seconds
to 2.4. *Best route.* Bill raises the AI question himself, which is how it should arrive in this
account.

## Meeting 3 — Anita Raval

Available only to a learner who asked who owns analytics, or who reached branch C with Bill.

**Anita opens:** "Bill says there is a platform going in for the promise-date work. The board
has asked me twice what our AI plan is and I have been buying time. What does this give me?"

The expansion arrives because the platform is installed. Nobody pitched it. Route Anita on her cost, which is delivery capacity against a board
question she cannot answer.

## Scoring

Standard sheet, plus:

- **AI restraint.** No Akka product named until Marguerite's cost was quantified, two points.
  Named in the first three minutes, zero.
- **The estimate.** Conceded before any counter-proposal, two points. Argued, zero, and the
  scenario fails.
- **Scope.** Reduced to a slice that fits the window, two points. Offered the whole rebuild
  faster, zero.
- **The funded line.** The $1.8 million and the eleven-week expiry surfaced, two points.
- **Week seven.** Answered with operating ownership before any capability, two points.

## Debrief

1. Marguerite told you she does not have an AI problem. What did you sell her?
2. Owen's fourteen months was correct. How did you get to six weeks without telling him he
   was wrong?
3. Who first said the word AI in this account, and at which meeting?
4. What does Akka actually get out of a consulting engagement priced as consulting?

The lesson to land: the wedge is any system the customer already needs and cannot get built,
and it is often better when no AI is involved, because nobody is evaluating an AI platform
and there is no committee. A funded line that expires, an estimate measured in quarters, and
an operations leader who owns the delay are the conditions. The platform gets installed. The account watches delivery run at a speed they had priced at a year, and the agentic conversation starts from inside.
