# Scenario 8 — Thirty minutes, no briefing

**Entry solution (facilitator only):** Akka Specify. The learner must derive it, because the
buyer uses none of the trigger sentences.
**Teaches:** Translating a business outcome into one of the four costs. Working inside a
partner's relationship without taking it over.
**Time:** 40 minutes to run, 20 to debrief.

---

## Account brief

A Deloitte principal you have worked with twice has an account she wants you in. She sends
one message the night before:

> "Meeting is 30 minutes tomorrow at 8am with the COO. She is sharp and she does not do
> technology conversations. I have not briefed her on Akka. Please follow my lead on the
> commercial side. — Ellen"

You know the account is a US property and casualty insurer. You know nothing else.

## Cast

**Ellen Vasquez, Deloitte principal.** Owns the relationship. Six years in this account.

**Rochelle Adeyemi, Chief Operating Officer.** Owns claims, underwriting operations, and
customer service. 4,000 people report through her.

**Nate Brolin, VP Claims Technology.** Not in the meeting.

### Facilitator only

The insurer writes about $2.8 billion in personal auto and homeowners premium.

Rochelle's problem, stated in her own terms: average claims cycle time is nine days and her
board target is two. She has said this in three consecutive quarterly reviews. She has no
technical vocabulary and will not use any of the phrases in the routing tables.

What is actually between her and two days: her team has documented the target process in
detail. Nate's technology group has a two-year backlog and can start this in Q3 of next year.
The design is known and the build capacity is not there. **The gap is rework and delivery capacity, which is Akka Specify.** A learner who routes to SDK is solving a problem nobody
has.

Ellen's position: Deloitte's own build practice has recommended a custom implementation on
this account, and Ellen thinks it will take eighteen months and lose the client's patience.
She wants a faster path and she cannot be seen undercutting her own firm. **If the learner
proposes Akka delivering instead of Deloitte, Ellen stops the meeting afterwards and the
account closes.**

Rochelle's hidden line, delivered if asked what is between her and the target: "Nate says Q3
next year. My board target is this year."

## The meeting — Rochelle and Ellen

**Ellen opens:** "Rochelle, this is who I mentioned. They work on the systems side of what we
have been discussing." Then she stops talking. She will not rescue the learner.

**Rochelle:** "I have thirty minutes. Ellen thinks you can help with claims cycle time. We are
at nine days and the board wants two. Go ahead."

### The decision

Rochelle has stated an outcome and named no cost. The learner must translate it before routing, and
guessing wrong burns the meeting.

### Branches (facilitator only)

**A. Assumes a runtime problem and pitches the SDK.** Rochelle does not follow and does not
pretend to. She checks her phone at minute twelve.

**B. Delivers the 90-second platform statement first.** Rochelle listens. The statement costs 90 of her 1,800 seconds and answers a question she did not ask. Recoverable, but expensive here.

**C. Asks what is between her and two days.** Rochelle names the Q3 backlog and the board
deadline. *Correct route.* The learner now knows the constraint is build capacity.

**D. Branch C, then asks whether the target process is designed or still being decided.**
Rochelle confirms it is documented in detail. *Best route.* Design is done, build is blocked, and
Akka Specify is exactly the shape of that problem.

The diagnostic question the module teaches: is the gap knowing what to build, or getting it
built? Rochelle's answer is the second, and it decides the route.

### The commercial move

Whatever branch, the learner must place Deloitte in the delivery.

**A. Proposes Akka delivers.** Ellen intervenes politely and the account is lost afterwards.

**B. Proposes the customer-owned factory operated by Deloitte's team.** *Correct route.* Ellen
relaxes visibly, which Rochelle notices.

**C. Leaves the delivery question unanswered.** Ellen has to raise it herself, which reads as the
learner not having thought about it.

### The second cost

Claims decisions are adverse-action decisions on natural persons. Governance is the correct
second cost. The right person is not Nate. Ask Rochelle who signs off that an automated claims
decision is allowed, and let her name the role.

## After the meeting — Ellen

**Ellen:** "That went well. What do you need from me, and what do I do about my own build
practice?"

The learner should register the opportunity per the deal registration process in the
partner enablement brief, and should answer Ellen's second question honestly:
the customer-owned factory model gives Deloitte's build practice the work, delivered faster,
across the ten other processes Rochelle owns.

## Scoring

Standard sheet, plus:

- **Translation.** Derived the cost from Rochelle's outcome by asking, two points. Guessed
  correctly, one. Guessed wrong, zero.
- **Partner.** Deloitte placed in the delivery unprompted, two points. Prompted by Ellen, one.
  Displaced, zero, and the scenario fails.
- **Time.** A dated next step inside thirty minutes, two points.

## Debrief

1. Rochelle used none of the sentences in the routing table. What did you ask to find the
   cost?
2. When did you decide this was not a runtime problem, and what told you?
3. Ellen said to follow her lead commercially. What did you do with that instruction?

The lesson to land: business executives describe outcomes, and outcomes map to more than one
cost. One diagnostic question separates knowing what to build from getting it built, and that
question is the entire routing decision in an operations meeting. In a partner-sourced deal,
the partner's position inside the delivery is not a detail to settle later, because it decides
whether there is a second meeting at all.
