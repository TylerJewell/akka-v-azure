Base-case annual benefit of the programme

The pricing runs off POS Malaysia's own parcel records. 23,790,551 parcels across 170 days were joined to the July 2026 revenue-per-item extract and the customer master file, then grouped into 28-day windows so that each account can be compared with itself and with the network around it.

The long-tail book holds every account shipping 50 parcels a month or fewer. It bills RM 21,673,649 a year. Across the most recent eight weeks the total barely moves. Across the full five and a half months the same accounts give up RM 1,684,949 of annual run-rate, a fall of 15% a year.

Underneath the total, two large amounts of money move in opposite directions. RM 6,965,534 of annual run-rate leaves accounts that shipped less, and RM 7,944,196 arrives from accounts that shipped more. Neither movement appears on a monthly revenue report, and no one is acting on either one. The initiatives set out below work on those two movements.

What we propose

POS Malaysia works each account according to the movement its own shipping records show. An account that drops a volume band is contacted in the same 28 days it drops. An account that grows is asked for more. An account that outgrows the long tail is handed to a person. None of these accounts receives any of that today.

Catch slides early and rank up to a person carry RM 1,413,323 of the RM 2,071,827, which is 68% of the base case. Both run on lists of a few hundred accounts, and both are worked by relationship managers POS Malaysia already employs. Ask growers for more and win back the silent run account by account under automation. Campaign the sparse runs by group.

Software prepares every one of these. It reads the account records each morning, works out which accounts moved, states the likely cause, drafts the message, and holds it for a person to approve before anything is sent.

The benefit is annual run-rate, not booked revenue. It is what the accounts would bill in a year if they held the volume the initiative aims at. Whether the intervention moves that volume is settled by holding a matched set of accounts back and leaving them alone, and comparing the two sets after two windows. That comparison is what replaces the assumed recovery rates with rates measured on POS Malaysia's own book.

Definitions

  • Parcel

    One consignment in the AXIS records. Volume throughout this pricing is a count of parcels, not a count of orders or shipments.

  • ARPI

    Average revenue per item. The average ringgit POS Malaysia bills a given account for one parcel. Every revenue figure is a parcel count multiplied by that account's own ARPI.

  • Run-rate

    What an account would bill in a year if it kept shipping at the rate it shipped in the last 28 days. It makes a 28-day count comparable to an annual budget.

  • Volume band

    A bracket of parcels per 28 days. Lost is zero, Minimal is 1 to 5, Low is 6 to 15, Mid is 16 to 46, High is 47 or more. An account moving between two bands is the event the programme acts on.

  • Window

    A block of 28 days. The extract holds six consecutive windows. The three most recent are named P1, P2 and P3, with P3 ending 30 June 2026.

  • The long tail

    Every account shipping 50 parcels a month or fewer. POS Malaysia has 14,843 accounts shipping anything at all, and 11,733 of them fall inside that ceiling.

Most of the money leaves while the customer is still a customer

A small shipper rarely stops in one step. The usual pattern runs from high volume to low volume, then a stretch of low volume, and only then silence. Treating a customer as either active or gone misses most of the money.

Of the 8,981 accounts shipping at the start of the window, 2,501 dropped at least one volume band, giving up RM 5,483,872 of annual run-rate. Customers who left account for 24% of that leak. Customers who stayed and shipped less account for 76%.

The test claims 51% of the decline

Shipping volumes wobble on their own. To separate a real drop from a wobble, each account is first compared with the network. If the whole network shipped 3% less this window, every account is expected to ship 3% less, and an account is only flagged when it falls further than that. The size of the gap is then weighed against how large the account's numbers are. A fall from 200 parcels to 150 is large enough to flag. A fall from 4 parcels to 3 sits inside ordinary variation. An account is flagged only when a gap that size would happen by chance less than once in twenty times.

Rank up to a person is the largest single initiative

315 accounts ended the window in the High band, shipping 47 parcels or more per 28 days, and they average RM 15,782 a year each. They receive no dedicated account management. They sit inside the long-tail automation with everyone else. Assumptions that would need to be revisited

  • Band changes near a threshold

    An account shipping 16 parcels in one window and 15 in the next crosses from Mid to Low on a single parcel. Some share of band changes is threshold noise of this kind. The pilot's control group is what separates the genuine effect from it.

  • The success rates

    The rates come from published business-to-business retention practice, not from measurement on a postal and courier long tail. The ordering of the initiatives against each other is well supported. The specific percentages are directional and are labelled as such. No unattributed benchmark is used anywhere in the pricing.

  • The customer master file

    Lookup_Customer is dated 16 February 2022 and is applied to 2026 transactions. 7.9% of parcels in the AXIS extract map to no master account, and roughly half of active accounts carry no CRM attributes. A refreshed extract is the single most valuable input POS Malaysia could supply.

  • Revenue per item

    A single July 2026 figure is applied across every window, so price is held flat and every trend reported here is driven by volume. A declining account that is also being discounted is worse than shown. Monthly history would separate the two effects.

  • Six months of history

    The windows include Chinese New Year and Hari Raya. Seasonality is handled by measuring each account against the network rather than against its own past. A full year would allow that adjustment to be verified directly.

How the programme is built

Each initiative is served by its own piece of software. Each one reads the account records, decides what to do, drafts the message in words, and waits for a person to approve it before anything is sent. They share one set of foundations, so account history, daily work queues and outcome tracking are built once.

Built once and shared

  • Daily refresh

    Runs the pipeline behind these figures once a day and publishes the band movements and risk scores every other part reads.

  • Account record

    One record per account holding band history, contact history, what was tried, what happened, and who owns the account today. Every change is appended in order rather than overwritten, so the account's full history can be replayed at any date.

  • Message drafter

    Takes the chosen play and the account's own history and writes the outreach. The software reads the account's shipping pattern and produces a message that refers to it, in the way a person would if they had time to read every account.

  • Approval queue

    Holds any message above a value threshold until a person approves it. One queue covers every initiative, so an approver sees a single list.

  • Outcome tracker

    Watches the following windows and records whether the account recovered or grew. Those results are what replace the assumed recovery rates with measured ones.

  • Work queue screen

    The screen a relationship manager opens each morning, listing the day's accounts with the reason each one appeared and the recommended action.

Rank up to a person

Triggers when an account reaches the High band. It reads the shipping history, rhythm, revenue per item, product mix, industry and any recorded service events, and writes a one-page handover briefing. A fixed rule assigns the account to a relationship manager by territory, industry and current workload. The manager is notified, accepts, receives the briefing, and first contact is tracked. Nothing is sent to the customer here. The work is to make the relationship manager useful on day one.

Catch slides early

Triggers on any downward band move where the account is still shipping. It reads the shape of the decline, whether the gaps between shipments are widening, whether the drop was sudden or steady, whether the routes changed, and how many days the account was active, and states the most likely cause. A fixed rule then picks one of four plays: a service check-in, a retention offer, a rate-card review, or a short questionnaire. The message is drafted against the chosen play and the account's own volume history. High-value accounts go to a person for approval. Low-value accounts send directly within pre-approved wording. The next window's volume decides whether the account is closed as recovered or escalated to a person.

Ask growers for more

Triggers on any upward band move short of High, which routes to rank up to a person instead. It compares the account with similar accounts that grew further and suggests what to offer: additional services, a better rate card, adjacent routes, or a vehicle wrap for accounts on dedicated pickup. It drafts an approach that opens on the growth the account has already shown. Opens, clicks and replies are tracked, and strong engagement escalates to a person. An account approaching High-band volumes hands off to rank up to a person.

Campaign the sparse

Groups the 2,207 sparse accounts by industry, location and service tier. It picks the group, the angle and the offer based on how earlier campaigns performed, then sends to hundreds of accounts at once. Some accounts in each group are deliberately left out and not contacted. Comparing the two sets afterwards shows what the campaign did rather than what the market did. Any sparse account that climbs a band moves into the ask-growers queue. Approval works differently here: a campaign is approved once and then runs at scale, rather than message by message.

Win back the silent

Triggers when an account reaches the Lost band from a band above it, with a minimum silence before it fires so that a quiet season does not set it off. It reads the pattern before the silence and any CRM context and states a likely cause: a service problem, a competitor win, a closure, or a project that ended. Contact runs on a fixed cadence of an initial approach, a follow-up at two weeks and a final at six weeks, after which the account is closed as confirmed lost. Each touch takes a different angle, and open-text replies are turned into structured feedback for service and product. Win back the silent carries the tightest approval controls, because the message goes to a customer who has already stopped buying.

What it runs on

The programme is built on the Akka Agentic AI Platform. Each initiative is a sequence of steps that the platform remembers, so a step that fails resumes where it stopped rather than starting over. Account records, work queues and the integrations into the relationship-manager application and the campaign tool are platform components. A separate architecture document covers the component design.

Appendix A. How the numbers are derived

Windows of 28 days rather than calendar months

Volume in this book depends heavily on the day of the week. Monday runs about 5.6 times Sunday. A period that is not a whole number of weeks holds a different mix of weekdays from the period beside it, and that difference is larger than most real trends. 28 days is the shortest period holding every weekday the same number of times, so the day mix cannot distort a comparison. Six of them cover 168 days, about five and a half months.

Days removed from the extract

Ten days in the six-month AXIS extract contain between 1 and 29 parcels network-wide, and one day is missing entirely. An ordinary working day carries about 145,000 parcels. A public holiday depresses volume and still leaves thousands: Chinese New Year registered about 3,700. Single-digit days are failed extracts. Any day below 15% of the trailing median is masked rather than deleted, recorded in the coverage log, and removed from its window's day count. Every window is then normalised back to 28 clean days. Reading a failed extract as real demand would drive a network-wide false decline and trigger retention contact against the entire book.

The test that flags an account

An account's expected volume for the current window is its previous window scaled by how the network moved. Observed volume is compared with that expectation using a standard method for counting data that stays reliable when the counts are small. The threshold is set at the point where a gap that size would occur by chance about once in twenty times.

  • Falling back a window

    An account that collapsed from a large base, 242 parcels to 4 to 7, has too little recent volume to test. The baseline then falls back one window further. Without that fallback the account is filed as insufficient signal and sent to campaign nurture while it is plainly leaving. The collapse is the signal.

  • Guarding rank-up

    Growth is only treated as sustained when it rises across both windows and the starting window held at least 10 parcels. Without that guard, an account going 0 to 1 to 233 parcels passes the rising test while being a first order rather than a trend.

The daily risk score

Each account carries a risk score built from four inputs: how long it has been since the last shipment compared with that account's usual gap, which way its volume is moving, how recently it shipped at all, and how many days it was active. The four inputs carry weights of 40, 30, 20 and 10. Tested against history, the riskiest tenth and second tenth of the book together contain 74 of every 100 accounts that went on to stop shipping, and one in three accounts flagged did stop. The score drives the daily work queue. The initiatives are priced on band movement, not on the score.

The group left alone

The book moves on its own, so a treated set of accounts that grows has demonstrated nothing by itself. Holding a set of accounts back and leaving them alone is the only way to separate the programme's effect from market movement and seasonality without both parties having to agree on a model first. Account-level annual change has a mean of −RM 659 and a spread of RM 9,775, which is what sets the sample sizes.
