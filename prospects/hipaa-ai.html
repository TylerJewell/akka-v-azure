What HIPAA asks of a covered entity running AI agents

The HIPAA rules and the HHS and CMS AI guidance carry obligations for an AI system that touches protected health information. Together they resolve to 25 controls.

Sector Healthcare payers and providers Jurisdiction United States Applies to Covered entities and business associates

The HIPAA Security, Privacy and Breach Notification Rules at 45 CFR Part 164, together with the HHS AI Strategy, the CMS AI Roadmap and the CMS prior-authorization rule CMS-0057-F, set out what a covered entity owes the Office for Civil Rights when it runs AI models and agents on protected health information. Those instruments resolve to 25 discrete controls. CMS-0057-F took effect in January 2026. OMB records the final action on the proposed Security Rule update as due in July 2027.

Protected health information is the subject of most of the obligation. §164.502(b) limits each use and disclosure to the minimum necessary for the purpose it serves. The set of 25 carries nine controls that transform the payload before it leaves the agent. An agent that reads a full patient record and passes it to a tool has made a disclosure, and the rule reaches that disclosure the same way it reaches a fax.

HIPAA differs from supervisory guidance in how it is enforced. The Office for Civil Rights administers tiered civil money penalties reaching $1.9M per violation category per calendar year, and an investigation opens on a complaint or a breach report. The investigation asks a covered entity for records of what its systems did and when.

What the rules ask a covered entity to do

  • Disclose the minimum necessary and nothing more

    §164.502(b) limits each use and disclosure to what the purpose requires. §164.514 sets the Safe Harbor identifiers that must be removed for data to leave as de-identified. §164.512 applies the same minimum-necessary limit to disclosures permitted without authorization. §164.522 gives an individual the right to request a restriction that the covered entity has agreed to honour. §164.524 gives an individual access to their own record with third-party information removed.

  • Hold the record inside the technical safeguards

    §164.312 requires access control with unique user identification and automatic logoff, audit controls that record and examine system activity, integrity controls that authenticate electronic PHI, and transmission security over the network. A system processing PHI keeps a security architecture document covering encryption at rest, encryption in transit, access control, audit logging and transmission security, together with a matrix specifying role-based permissions.

  • Keep the business associate inside its agreement

    §164.504(e) requires a written agreement that names the permitted uses and disclosures before a business associate receives PHI. An agent calling a hosted model, a plugin or an inference API is disclosing to that party, so PHI outside the scope of the agreement is removed before the call is made.

  • Tell the patient an AI system was involved

    The HHS AI Strategy §4.1 and the CMS AI Roadmap §2.3, read with the notice of privacy practices at §164.520, require a patient to receive disclosure of AI use and notice of the right to review before a clinical AI decision affects them.

  • Decide prior authorization inside the timeframes

    42 CFR §422.568, as amended by CMS-0057-F, sets 72 hours for an expedited decision and 7 calendar days for a standard one. A denial carries clinical rationale and appeal rights. The decision record names the clinical criteria section that was applied, and criteria are validated against current standards of care. A decision above the delegated authority tier goes to a physician reviewer.

  • Screen the provider and watch the billing pattern

    42 USC §1320a-7b requires exclusion screening of providers and prescribers against the OIG List of Excluded Individuals and Entities, a formal fraud, waste and abuse compliance programme, and an anomaly rate on suspicious billing kept within threshold.

  • Register the system and keep the policy current

    OMB M-24-10 §5(b) and the HHS AI Strategy §3.2 require a healthcare AI system to be recorded in the HHS AI use-case inventory with its clinical scope. §164.530(c) requires a documented sanitisation and minimisation policy for PHI, maintained as the systems it governs change. 42 CFR §422.101 and §422.562 require a coverage AI system to publish its coverage criteria specification and a documented appeal process.

HIPAA and the CMS rules account for 25 of those controls. The United States carries more beyond them. Washington's My Health My Data Act adds 4 controls over consumer health data that falls outside HIPAA's scope. FDA guidance on AI and machine-learning-enabled device software, with its good machine learning practice principles, adds 10 controls where a system supports a clinical decision. HITRUST CSF adds 4 where a payer or a provider requires certification of its business associates. Each becomes relevant as a covered entity writes business or holds operations in those areas.

Automatic enforcement for runtime controls

The 11 controls that resolve to a transformation of the payload or a scheduled score are enforced by the runtime alongside the agent. A sanitizer at the model request and at the tool boundary applies the minimum-necessary limit, the Safe Harbor identifier set, the scope of the business associate agreement and any standing restriction an individual has requested. Masking applies where PHI would otherwise cross an unencrypted tool boundary. Scheduled evaluations carry the security event rate per million operations on a 7-day rolling window and the suspicious billing anomaly rate. Each verdict is recorded in the same event-sourced log as the action it governed, which is what makes it evidence.

Pipeline execution for controls outside the runtime

The 14 controls satisfied at build and release time run in the delivery pipeline. TestKit asks whether the system behaves correctly, EvalKit asks whether it performs against quality rubrics and whether it has regressed, and both run against real cases in the pipeline and against historical production data. Each run produces a dated record tied to the matrix row it satisfies, which is the artifact an OCR investigation asks to see. Prior-authorization timeliness under §422.568 is measured this way against production decision history.

Tracing and retention

Akka traces every agentic AI interaction automatically. The trace carries the rendered prompt, the model and its version, the tools that were called, the authorization in force at the time, and the verdict of each control that ran. §164.312(b) requires audit controls that record and examine activity involving PHI, and §164.316(b)(2)(i) requires the documentation to be retained for six years. Retention is tiered, so recent traces stay immediately queryable and older ones compress into archive at a fraction of the cost, which keeps a six-year history affordable. An OCR request that asks what a model did on a given day is answered from the record itself.
