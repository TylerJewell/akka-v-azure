Partnership Exploration

Akka + Kyivstar: sovereign agentic AI, delivered.

A hosted Akka platform inside Kyivstar's datacenters — serverless for its SMBs, private installations for its enterprise accounts, operated as a sovereign service across its territories.

Partner · Kyivstar Model · OEM / Hosted + Private Install Territories · All Kyivstar markets

This is an overview of a potential partnership between Akka and Kyivstar for Kyivstar to host the Akka Agentic AI Platform in its own datacenters and deliver it to its 100,000 business customers — a multi-tenant serverless environment for the 95,000 SMBs, and private, single-tenant installations of Akka Automated Operations (AAO) for its larger 5,000 enterprise accounts. Data and compute stay resident in-country across the territories Kyivstar serves. Akka installs and maintains the platform remotely and, optionally, eventually enables Kyivstar's ops teams to manage the installations independently.

How it works — two delivery models

SMB · 95,000 accounts

Serverless, multi-tenant

Kyivstar hosts a shared, multi-tenant Akka environment — similar to what Akka runs at akka.io — with the billing model tailored to Kyivstar's requirements. Services from many customers run concurrently on shared compute and data infrastructure, a high-density model that keeps per-tenant cost low; customers self-serve through Kyivstar's portal.

This port would require more significant R&D alignment between the two teams than standing up the private AAO installation.

Enterprise · named accounts

Private AAO installation

For enterprise accounts that require dedicated data and traffic isolation, Akka installs a single-tenant AAO region inside Kyivstar's (or the customer's) cloud account — the same platform, with full multi-region HA/DR and independent scaling.

Single-tenant to Kyivstar, but multi-tenanted for the customer: many AI projects, developers, and services run within the one install. Persistence storage can be further isolated per service so those services' data is partitioned from one another inside the region.

Two operating options: Akka maintains each environment remotely per account, or Akka trains and enables Kyivstar's SREs to operate and maintain the installations independently. The second option requires close coordination — Kyivstar's team would parrot Akka's fleet-management operations across hundreds of installations.

Agentic AI: what the platform delivers, what Kyivstar brings

Akka is the agentic runtime — the components that build, run, and govern agents, and with the Optimize extension it also routes model traffic and generates and hosts small language models (SLMs). Kyivstar supplies the heavy substrate — the GPU fleet, foundation-model training, and MLOps — and can host models the gateway steers to. The AAO overview details the full component set; the essentials:

In the Akka platform

  • Akka SDK — the full toolchain to design, build, and test agentic systems: SDK, CLI, MCP server, local console, and testkit.
  • Akka Specify — spec-driven delivery: describe what the system must do, and generate the agents, workflows, APIs, memory, integrations, guardrails, evaluations, and tests that deliver it.
  • Agents — model-driven components with session memory and function tools, built against Akka's model provider interface. Akka would help develop and maintain model providers for each local-language LLM under consideration.
  • Autonomous agents & multi-agent coordination — durable decision loops, delegation, and hand-off without orchestration code.
  • Workflows — durable, long-running orchestration that resumes rather than restarts.
  • Durable memory & state — event-sourced entities and views, active-active replicated across regions.
  • Tools & MCP — MCP client and server, function tools, and model gateways.
  • Endpoints, streaming, timers, consumers — everything agents use to talk to the outside world.
  • Governance — evaluations, guardrails, policies, and a durable evidence record.
  • Optimize (extension) — the AI gateway that routes and steers model traffic by cost, performance, or task, plus the services to identify SLM candidates from that traffic, train new SLMs, and run inference for open-weight models on Kubernetes.

Kyivstar brings (the AI/ML substrate)

  • GPU reservations — accelerated compute capacity for inference and training.
  • Foundation-model training & fine-tuning — building and tuning the large models Akka's gateway routes to.
  • MLOps — model serving, registry, and lifecycle management at scale.
  • Foundation models — the LLMs and their hosting; Akka's gateway calls and steers to them.
  • Semantic storage — vector / embedding stores for retrieval.
  • Graph storage — knowledge-graph and relationship stores.

What Akka provides — and what it does not

Akka provides

  • The Akka Agentic AI Platform — SDK, CLI, MCP server, and the durable, elastic runtime for building and running agents.
  • AAO managed regions installed inside Kyivstar's datacenter — both the multi-tenant serverless environment and single-tenant private installs.
  • Day-2 operations: runtime patching, rolling zero-downtime updates, elastic autoscaling, HA/DR, and cross-region failover — installed and actively maintained remotely.
  • Multi-tenancy for SMB density and single-tenant isolation for enterprise, from one platform.
  • Sovereignty controls: data resident in Kyivstar's environment, encryption at rest and in transit, mTLS zero-trust between regions, IAM integration.
  • Observability via the Control Tower and forwarding to Kyivstar's chosen vendor.
  • A regulator-approved runtime already deployed in financial institutions under supervision.

Akka does not provide

  • Datacenter or cloud infrastructure. Kyivstar supplies the compute, storage, and network the platform runs on.
  • The customer-facing portal / marketplace and metering-to-invoice for the 100,000 businesses — Kyivstar builds and owns it.
  • The agents themselves. Applications are built by customers, Kyivstar, or SIs on the SDK; Akka supplies the platform, not the use cases.
  • End-customer L1/L2 support and onboarding — Kyivstar fronts the customer relationship; Akka provides L3 platform support.

Who manages what

The cleanest way to frame roles is by the interfaces each team touches. Kyivstar owns the customer relationship and the experience around it; its ops teams run the day-to-day; Akka's SRE teams keep the platform itself healthy.

Ops maturity path. With enough experience and training, Kyivstar's internal ops team could over time take on the Akka SRE functions — installation, patching, HA/DR, and failover — running the fleet independently under Akka's coordination.

Go-to-market. Akka frequently co-sells with its partners, and we would expect the same here: explaining the state of agentic AI to the market, helping sell to the customer against their specific business needs, and co-delivering custom POCs for large enterprises that need to see the system working before they buy.

On branding: the customer-facing layer — portal, storefront, onboarding, billing — is Kyivstar's to design and brand, with co-branding open to discussion. White-labeling the developer toolchain (SDK, CLI, MCP server) and the platform-management interfaces is possible but not the default: producing Kyivstar-flavored builds of that tooling is an Akka engineering effort that has to be scoped separately, as it requires significant custom R&D. The base case ships the Akka-branded toolchain.

Architecture, in brief

Today Akka separates a global Federation Plane (accounts, billing metadata, deployment coordination, operated by Akka) from the Application Planes that run workloads inside Kyivstar's infrastructure. Application data never leaves the application plane, and if the Federation Plane is unavailable, running applications continue uninterrupted. Within the next 6–9 months Akka will ship a version of AAO that requires no Federation Plane at all — a fully self-contained installation, which further strengthens the sovereignty story for Kyivstar's territories. There are more details on how it works in the accompanying AAO Technical Overview.

Installation and maintenance

Akka installs a region into a prepared cloud account, provisioning networking, DNS, a Kubernetes cluster, an encrypted persistence store, a registry, and the Akka operators — then patches and upgrades it through a recurring maintenance window. Environment provisioning and ongoing configuration are driven by a Crossplane-based control substrate.

To date, that substrate has been engineered against the VPCs of the major hyperscalers (AWS, GCP, Azure). Installing into Kyivstar's own datacenter and cloud fabric is an engineering effort: the Crossplane providers and environment-management substrate would have to be adapted to Kyivstar's infrastructure primitives. Scoping that adaptation is a required early step and a gating item for timeline.

Commercial constructs to explore

  • OEM / wholesale pricing

    Kyivstar acquires platform capacity at a wholesale rate and resells to its customers at its own price — Akka is not a party to the SMB revenue. Wholesale basis to define: consumption (throughput / compute) versus per-active-tenant capacity blocks.

  • Private-install licensing (enterprise)

    Per-region license plus an annual operations-and-support fee for each single-tenant AAO installation, with Kyivstar as reseller of record to the enterprise account.

  • Operating model & support tiering

    Choose per deployment: Akka operates each environment remotely, or Akka trains and certifies Kyivstar's SREs to run the fleet themselves — the latter structured as enablement plus ongoing coordination. Either way, Kyivstar fronts L1/L2 to end customers and Akka provides L3.

  • Territory, term & sovereignty commitments

    Scope of territories (all markets Kyivstar operates in), exclusivity if any, contract term, and written commitments that data and compute remain resident in-country per territory.

  • Branding & IP boundaries

    Scope of co-branding at Kyivstar's portal layer and trademark and usage terms; any white-labeling of the SDK, CLI, MCP, or platform would require a more involved conversation.
