What OSFI asks of an insurer running AI agents

Five OSFI instruments carry AI obligations for a federally regulated insurer. Together they resolve to 61 controls.

Sector Property & casualty insurance Jurisdiction Canada Applies to Federally regulated insurers

Guidelines E-23, E-21, B-13 and B-10, together with OSFI's two Technology Risk Bulletins, set out what a federally regulated insurer owes its supervisor when it runs AI models and agents. Those five instruments resolve to 61 discrete controls. E-21 reaches full adherence on 1 September 2026, and E-23 becomes enforceable on 1 May 2027.

The bulletins are the most operationally specific AI material OSFI has published, and the Generative and Agentic AI bulletin is the only Canadian regulatory text that addresses agentic AI directly. The bulletin names autonomous reasoning and execution, over-privileged agent access, unvalidated downstream actions, and multi-step exfiltration through tools and APIs.

OSFI guidelines are supervisory guidance. Conformance is addressed through supervisory ratings, staging and remediation expectations, so the work is measured by the evidence an institution can produce on request.

What the guidelines ask an insurer to do

  • Know every model in use

    E-23 requires a defined identification process that surveys the enterprise, triages models whose inherent risk is non-negligible, assigns each a risk rating, and records it in an evergreen enterprise inventory. The inventory covers vendor models and decommissioned models.

  • Validate before deployment, and keep validating

    E-23 runs the full lifecycle: development standards, independent pre-deployment review of conceptual soundness, data suitability for the intended use, controlled deployment with production-parity testing, and monitoring standards with breach thresholds that handle drift and autonomy. Review depth, approval authority and monitoring frequency all scale with the model's risk rating.

  • Keep the agent inside its authority

    The Generative and Agentic AI bulletin gives each agent a unique non-human identity with least-privilege, just-in-time credentials, restricts tool usage to an allow-list mediated by a gateway, pauses high-impact actions for human approval, keeps an accountable human on material decisions, and logs agent activity and tool usage for anomaly review.

  • Deliver the operations E-21 designates through disruption

    E-21 designates the operations whose failure would materially impair an institution, its depositors or the financial system. Each designated operation is mapped with its dependencies and given a tolerance for disruption. Scenario testing shows the operation persists within that tolerance. The Generative and Agentic AI bulletin applies the same expectations to AI, mapping AI dependencies to the designated operations, testing failure scenarios, and keeping manual fallbacks available.

  • Govern the AI supply chain

    B-10 covers pre-contract and periodic risk assessment, subcontracting, written agreements, data security, audit and information rights, continuity, and ongoing monitoring. Applied to AI, this reaches hosted models, plugins and inference APIs, and it carries the expectations on AI concentration risk and on disclosing AI's part in service delivery.

  • Operate the technology estate

    Cutting across all of these concerns is an operating spine including named senior accountability, asset inventory, change and release management, patching, incident management, disaster recovery and cyber controls. B-13 sets those expectations.

Where each control is satisfied

There are 61 controls that must be implemented at different parts of your SDLC.

OSFI accounts for 61 of those controls. Canada carries more beyond OSFI, with PIPEDA and ISED's voluntary code of conduct for generative AI adding 12 controls over personal data and model transparency. Insurance supervision elsewhere adds further obligations, including the IAIS Insurance Core Principles that OSFI applies as an IAIS member, the NAIC model bulletin on insurer use of AI, New York DFS Circular Letter 7 on underwriting and pricing, Colorado's regulation on external consumer data, and EIOPA's AI governance principles. Each becomes relevant as an insurer writes business or holds operations in those jurisdictions.

Automatic enforcement for runtime controls

The 15 controls that resolve to observation or to a decision in the moment are enforced by the runtime alongside the agent. Authorization at the model request and the tool boundary carries agent identity, the tool allow-list and trusted input sources. A workflow pause holds an action for human approval, which is the bulletin's approval checkpoint and its accountable human on material decisions. Evaluators score every interaction in the background for agent activity logging, prompt and output monitoring, AI-enabled detection and technology incident management. Scheduled evaluations carry access recertification, patch currency, third-party monitoring, risk reporting and E-23 drift monitoring. Each verdict is recorded in the same event-sourced log as the action it governed, which is what makes it evidence.

Pipeline execution for controls outside the runtime

The 46 controls satisfied at build and release time run in the delivery pipeline. TestKit asks whether the system behaves correctly, EvalKit asks whether it performs against quality rubrics and whether it has regressed, and both run against real cases in the delivery pipeline and against historical production data. Each run produces a dated record tied to the matrix row it satisfies, which is the artifact an OSFI examination asks to see. E-21 scenario testing and the bulletin's AI failure-scenario testing use the same machinery pointed at disruption conditions.

Tracing and retention

Akka traces every agentic AI interaction automatically. The trace carries the rendered prompt, the model and its version, the tools that were called, the authorization in force at the time, and the verdict of every control that ran. Retention is tiered, so recent traces stay immediately queryable and older ones compress into archive at a fraction of the cost, which keeps petabyte-scale history affordable. An OSFI examination that asks what a model did on a given day is answered from the record itself.
