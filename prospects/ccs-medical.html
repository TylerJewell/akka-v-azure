What US law asks of a supplier running an AI calling agent

Six instruments carry obligations for a covered entity running a patient-facing voice agent. Together they resolve to 39 controls.

Industry Durable medical equipment Jurisdiction United States, 50 states Prepared for CCS Medical

HIPAA, the CMS fraud and abuse statutes, PCI DSS 4.0, the Texas Responsible Artificial Intelligence Governance Act, the Utah AI Policy Act and Colorado's automated-decision statute set out what a durable medical equipment supplier owes when a voice agent speaks to its patients. Those six instruments resolve to 39 discrete controls. Twenty-two of the 39 are decided while the call is in progress.

HIPAA is the instrument CCS Medical already runs a programme against. The obligations that bind a voice agent most tightly come from two places outside it. The Telephone Consumer Protection Act governs whether the call may be placed at all. The Medicare DMEPOS supplier standards govern what the call may be about. The Telephone Consumer Protection Act carries statutory damages of $500 to $1,500 per call, and a DMEPOS breach carries revocation of Medicare billing privileges.

The Federal Communications Commission ruled on 8 February 2024 that AI-generated voices are artificial voices under the Telephone Consumer Protection Act. Every outbound call the agent places therefore requires prior express consent, and marketing content requires prior express written consent. The Act carries a private right of action of $500 to $1,500 per call. Consent has to be provable for each call that was placed.

A supplier that bills Medicare Part B is bound by the DMEPOS supplier standards at 42 CFR §424.57. Standard (c)(11) prohibits unsolicited telephone contact with a Medicare beneficiary. Contact is permitted where the beneficiary has given written consent, where the beneficiary is an existing customer being contacted about an item already purchased, and where the contact concerns a covered item currently being rented. The sanction for a breach is revocation of billing privileges.

What the instruments ask a supplier to do

  • Say that the caller is software

    The Utah AI Policy Act requires a supplier using generative AI in a regulated consumer transaction to disclose that use when the consumer asks. Section 5 of the Federal Trade Commission Act reaches a synthetic voice that leads a patient to believe they are speaking to a person. Colorado HB 26-1263 and the California BOT Act impose a proactive disclosure on operators of a conversational AI service, and the scope of both for a telephone agent is set out below.

  • Hold the consent before the call is placed

    The Telephone Consumer Protection Act requires prior express consent for an artificial-voice call and prior express written consent where the content is marketing. The DMEPOS supplier standards require written beneficiary consent, an existing customer relationship for the item in question, or a current rental. Both tests run before the dial. A record naming the patient, the item and the date of consent answers both tests.

  • Keep the call inside the subject it was authorised for

    The DMEPOS exception covers an item the beneficiary already purchases. A call that moves to a different product line falls outside it and puts billing privileges at risk. The treatment exemption under the Telephone Consumer Protection Act narrows on the same movement. The Fair Debt Collection Practices Act and Regulation F apply once the call turns to a balance owed, adding contact-frequency limits, calling-hour limits and required disclosures.

  • Protect what is said on the call

    Call audio, transcripts and derived summaries are Protected Health Information and enter the designated record set. The HIPAA Security Rule at 45 CFR §164.312 requires encryption at rest and in transit, access controls, audit logging and transmission security over all of it. PCI DSS 4.0 applies when a card number is spoken on a recorded line. All-party consent statutes in California, Washington, Illinois, Pennsylvania, Florida and Massachusetts require the patient's consent before recording begins.

  • Never present the agent as a clinician

    Tennessee SB 1580 prohibits representing an AI system as a qualified mental-health professional. Section 5 of the Federal Trade Commission Act reaches the same representation about clinical care. A patient who asks the agent a clinical question receives a referral to a person, and the agent holds no authority to answer it.

  • Produce the record afterwards

    The Telephone Consumer Protection Act is litigated call by call, and the defence is the consent record for the call that was placed. HIPAA gives a patient a right of access to the designated record set. A DMEPOS audit asks which beneficiary authorised which contact. A dated record tied to the individual call answers the call-by-call claim, the access request and the audit.

The distribution separates this system from most of the corpus. Across the 39 controls, 22 are decided while the call is in progress. A voice agent speaks to the patient, handles Protected Health Information and card data on the same line, and changes subject inside a single call. The obligations attach to the utterance.

global AI regulations in the corpus, carrying 1,254 controls

The six instruments account for 39 of those controls. State privacy and biometric law adds 50 more. The California Consumer Privacy Act, the Washington My Health My Data Act, and the privacy statutes of Texas, Virginia, Oregon, Iowa, Indiana and Montana carry exemptions for a covered entity that differ state by state, and several exempt Protected Health Information without exempting the entity that holds it. The Illinois Biometric Information Privacy Act carries 7 of the 50 and applies once a voiceprint is used to identify or authenticate a caller, with statutory damages of $1,000 to $5,000 per violation and a private right of action.

Obligations that turn on a scope question

Two state conversational-AI statutes reach a patient-facing voice agent on their face and carry exclusions that have to be tested against the enacted text before they are counted. Both statutes are excluded from the 39.

Colorado HB 26-1263 binds operators of a publicly accessible conversational AI service and applies from 1 January 2027, with the first Attorney General report due 1 July 2027. The statute counts each offending output as a separate violation. That section carries around eleven exclusions, and two of them reach this deployment: customer-support systems, and entities already regulated under HIPAA. A refill and reorder agent operated by a covered entity has a claim to both. Confirming that claim against C.R.S. § 6-1-1708 is the first item of legal work, because it decides ten controls and an annual filing.

The California BOT Act at Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 17941 requires bot disclosure where the communication incentivises a sale. The operative text reaches a person who uses a bot to communicate with another person in California online. A telephone call is not an online communication on the ordinary reading, which places a voice agent outside the section. The Act applies to any web chat or messaging channel CCS Medical runs alongside the phone line.

Automatic enforcement for runtime controls

The 22 controls that resolve to observation or to a decision in the moment are enforced by the runtime alongside the agent. A guardrail on the agent's first utterance carries the disclosure the Utah Act and the Federal Trade Commission Act require. Sanitizers mask Protected Health Information and card data on the wire, which is the HIPAA minimum-necessary rule and the PCI DSS requirement on a recorded line. Authorization at the tool boundary carries the consent test, so a call to a patient whose record holds no prior express consent is refused before the dial. A workflow pause holds an action for human approval. Evaluators score each interaction in the background for subject drift, which is the DMEPOS boundary and the point where the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act attaches. Every decision and every verdict is recorded in the same event-sourced log as the action it governed, which is what makes it evidence.

Harness execution for controls outside the runtime

The 17 controls satisfied at build and release time run through the harness. TestKit asks whether the system behaves correctly, EvalKit asks whether it performs against quality rubrics and whether it has regressed, and both run against real cases in the delivery pipeline and against historical production data. Each run produces a dated record tied to the matrix row it satisfies, which is the artifact an Office for Civil Rights investigation asks to see. The subject-drift boundary that the DMEPOS standard draws is tested through the same machinery pointed at recorded calls that crossed it.
